01 / Their problem

You have the idea.

Maybe it's a product you need people to understand.

Maybe it's a campaign that needs to make an impression.

Maybe it's a story that exists as nothing more than a rough brief, a voice note, or a conversation.

The idea is there.

But the difficult part is turning it into something people actually want to watch — something that earns attention and makes people care.

That's where I come in.

I'll take your idea from the first question to the final frame.

I'll research what matters, help find the story, develop the visual language, and shape the final piece.

From the first thought to something people can't ignore.

02 / The difference

Why I work differently

Anyone can make something look good.

The difficult part is figuring out what it should say, who it should say it to, and what should make someone care enough to keep watching.

That's where I spend most of my effort.

01

I research before I create.

I don't like creating blindly.

Before I write a line, build a visual, or touch an edit, I want to understand what I'm working with.

I research the brand. I research the audience. I look at what competitors are doing, what has worked before, what hasn't, and what the campaign is actually trying to achieve.

Because the work can look incredible and still fail if it was built around the wrong idea.

I put the effort in before the audience ever sees the work.

02

I know how to write for people.

I've spent 4+ years ghostwriting essays and stories, including work that has gone on to win competitions.

That taught me something I carry into every campaign:

People don't connect with information. They connect with meaning.

A good story knows when to build tension, when to reveal something, when to slow down, and when to make the audience feel something before giving them the answer.

I bring that thinking into scripts, campaigns, short-form content and brand stories.

Because before someone buys the product, they have to care about it.

03

I know what it takes to make the idea real.

An idea is easy to have.

Execution is where things get difficult.

I've worked around real production sets and actively make films myself, so I understand that the final frame is only one part of the process.

There's planning, blocking, lighting, performance, camera, sound, continuity, logistics and a hundred small decisions that nobody notices when they're done properly.

That experience changed the way I approach visual work.

I don't just ask:

“Would this look cool?”

I ask:

“How would we actually make this work?”

04

I know how to find the moment.

Editing isn't about knowing where the buttons are.

I've spent the last two years learning how to find the moments that deserve to stay — and the ones that need to go.

A pause that makes a line land.

A cut that keeps someone watching.

A sound that makes a shot feel completely different.

A few frames removed that suddenly make the whole thing move.

That's the part of editing I care about:

Making every second earn its place.

I don't expect you to hire me because I say I'm good.
